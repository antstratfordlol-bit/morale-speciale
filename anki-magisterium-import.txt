How does the CDF Declaration *Iura et bona* (1980) define euthanasia in the strict sense (*in senso proprio*)? Provide the Latin/Italian essential formulation.	*"An action or an omission which of itself or by intention causes death, in order that all suffering may in this way be eliminated."*<br>(*Un'azione o un'omissione che di sua natura e nelle intenzioni procura la morte, allo scopo di eliminare ogni dolore.*)<br>- **Elements:** Commission (*actio*) or omission (*omissio*); intrinsic nature of the act (*de sua natura*) or intention of the agent (*in intentione*); motive of relieving suffering.
What moral attitude does the Instruction *Donum vitae* (I.1) require toward the human embryo from conception?	*"The human being must be respected and treated as a person from conception, and therefore from that same moment his rights as a person must be recognized, among which in the first place is the inviolable right of every innocent human being to life."*<br>(*L'essere umano va rispettato e trattato come una persona fin dal suo concepimento e, perciò, da quello stesso momento gli si devono riconoscere i diritti della persona, tra i quali anzitutto il diritto inviolabile di ogni essere umano innocente alla vita.*)
What is the decisive personalist criterion established by *Donum vitae* (II.7) to evaluate medical interventions on human procreation?	The distinction between **assistance** and **substitution**:<br>- **Licit:** The medical intervention acts as an **aid/assistance** (*ut actus coniugalis adiuvetur*) to the natural conjugal act so that it achieves its fruitful end.<br>- **Illicit:** The medical intervention **substitutes/replaces** (*ut actus coniugalis subrogetur*) the conjugal act (e.g. IVF, ICSI), transferring procreation to technological dominion (*dominium technicum*).
What are the three fundamental goods that any reproductive technique must safeguard according to *Dignitas personae* (n. 12)?	1. **The right to life and physical integrity** of every human being from conception to natural death.<br>2. **The unity of marriage**, which entails the mutual respect of the spouses' right to become father and mother only through each other.<br>3. **The specifically human values of sexuality**, which require that procreation be pursued as the fruit of the specific conjugal act of mutual spousal self-gift.
Why does *Dignitas personae* (nn. 18–19) declare the cryopreservation (*crioconservazione*) of human embryos morally illicit?	Because it is **incompatible with the respect owed to human embryos**:<br>1. Exposes human subjects to grave risks of death and physical injury during freezing and thawing.<br>2. Deprives them of maternal reception and gestation.<br>3. Leaves them in an existential limbo of abandonment (*embrioni orfani*), creating a factual injustice that is essentially irremediable (*situazione di ingiustizia di fatto insanabile*).
What did the CDF authoritatively determine in its 2007 *Responsum* regarding the administration of food and water to patients in a "vegetative state"?	1. **Obligatory:** The provision of water and food (even artificially) is an **ordinary and proportionate means** of preserving life and is morally obligatory as basic care, provided the body can assimilate it without causing significant physical discomfort.<br>2. **Cannot Be Discontinued:** It cannot be withdrawn simply because physicians determine with moral certainty that the patient will never regain consciousness. Withdrawal causes death by starvation/dehydration, constituting euthanasia by omission.
According to Pope St. John Paul II's 2000 Address to the Transplantation Society, what scientific criterion provides moral certainty of the death of a person?	The **complete and irreversible cessation of all brain activity** (**total brain death**, including both the cerebral cortex and the brainstem).<br>- *Theological basis:* Rigorously verified total brain death provides moral certainty that the somatic organism has permanently lost its unified integrative capacity, indicating that the rational soul has departed (*separatio animae a corpore*).<br>- *Rejection:* Cortical or "higher-brain" death formulations are rejected.
How did St. John Paul II characterize patients in a Persistent Vegetative State (PVS) in his 2004 Address to FIAMC?	1. They retain **full human personal dignity**; the term "vegetable" applied to a human being is fundamentally false and degrading.<br>2. Nutrition and hydration (even artificial) represent **natural, basic care**, not an extraordinary medical treatment.<br>3. Explicitly condemned the "quality of life" ethic that conditions human dignity on functional cognitive utility.
According to *Samaritanus bonus* (V.11), under what condition may sacraments (Penance, Anointing, Viaticum) be administered to a person requesting assisted suicide or euthanasia?	Sacraments **cannot be administered** so long as the person obstinately maintains their intention to commit euthanasia or assisted suicide.<br>- Sacramental absolution and anointing require sincere repentance and an explicit revocation of the illicit request. Administering them while the intention persists constitutes a simulation of the sacrament and a grave scandal.
In St. Thomas Aquinas's theology of legitimate defense, what does *praeter intentionem* mean, and how does it specify moral acts?	*Praeter intentionem* means **"outside or beside the intention"** (unintended side-effect).<br>- Moral actions receive their species strictly from what is directly intended (*ex eo quod intenditur*).<br>- In legitimate self-defense, the intended end is preserving one's life (*conservatio propriae vitae*); the death of the aggressor is *praeter intentionem* (accidental / *per accidens*), licit only if proportionate defensive force is used (*cum moderamine inculpatae tutelae*).
What thesis did secular bioethicist Ruth Macklin publish in the British Medical Journal (2003), and why is it rejected by Catholic personalism?	- **Thesis:** *"Dignity is a useless concept"* — claiming that dignity in bioethics is vacuous and means nothing more than **respect for personal autonomy**.<br>- **Personalist Refutation:** Equating dignity with actualized autonomy denies intrinsic dignity to non-autonomous human beings (embryos, fetuses, individuals with dementia, comatose patients), reducing them to disposable biological objects.
How does Prof. Pablo Requena distinguish between Ordinary vs. Extraordinary and Proportionate vs. Disproportionate means?	1. **Ordinary Means:** Always **Obligatory** (Lower threshold; below it lies *abbandono terapeutico*).<br>2. **Extraordinary Means:** **Optional** (non-obligatory, but licit if proportionate).<br>3. **Proportionate Means:** **Morally Licit** (reasonable proportion of burden to benefit).<br>4. **Disproportionate Means:** Always **Illicit** (Upper threshold; above it lies *accanimento terapeutico* / clinical futility).<br>- Between the two thresholds lies the vast **Space of Therapeutic Prudence**.
